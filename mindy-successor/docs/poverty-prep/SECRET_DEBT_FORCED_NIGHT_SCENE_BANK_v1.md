# Secret Debt Forced Night Scene Bank v1

Status: CONTENT_DRAFT scene bank for mandatory Night content while Secret Debt is authoritatively bound.

This file authors scene families only. It does not choose the NightFlow intercept, lodging-payment policy, Energy penalty, modifier composition, progression thresholds, or exact relationship effects.

## Fixed boundary

Already-directed structure:
- forced Secret Debt Night is mandatory while bound;
- it is separate from daytime hidden-career work;
- it occupies/suppresses incompatible ordinary voluntary Night freedom according to the final accepted Night policy;
- one Night advances calendar once;
- save/reload cannot duplicate the event or its effects;
- exact next-day pressure remains OPEN.

## Family A — late summons

### Setup

The heroine has finished the day.

Before the Night becomes hers, a message arrives:

> "Come back."

No explanation.

No request phrased as a request.

### Scene draft

The work waiting at night is smaller than a daytime shift and somehow more intrusive because of when it happens.

The point is not the task.

The point is that the heroine's time can be claimed after the day should have ended.

### Variants

- deliver a sealed note;
- wait for someone who is late;
- carry a small box across the district;
- unlock/close a room;
- copy a page from one ledger to another.

No variant changes mechanics by text alone.

## Family B — inventory after midnight

### Setup

A discrepancy has appeared in a ledger.

Someone decides it must be checked now.

### Scene draft

The heroine recounts shelves under dim light while the rest of the building has already settled.

A supervisor-like role may be present or absent.

Possible line:

> "It can wait until morning."
>
> "No. It can be done tonight."

### Variant tones

Compliance:
> You count carefully and finish without argument.

Resistance:
> You count carefully enough to make sure nobody can blame you twice.

No automatic relationship fact is implied.

## Family C — unwanted escort

### Setup

Someone important wants to cross town late.

The heroine is told to accompany them, carry something, or remain nearby.

### Scene draft

The city at night is familiar in the wrong way.

You are outside, moving freely enough to see the streets, and not freely enough to choose where you are going.

### Identity-neutral hooks

Possible future content may attach:
- route knowledge;
- overheard information;
- ally contact;
- resentment;
- trust.

None is mechanically granted by this draft.

## Family D — cleanup after a private gathering

### Setup

A room that was closed to the heroine earlier is suddenly her responsibility once everyone important has left.

### Scene draft

There are glasses, ashes, scraps of paper, and the kind of mess people stop seeing when they know someone else must clean it.

### Content opportunities

- overheard fragments;
- discarded note;
- recognizable seal;
- evidence of money movement.

These are optional future story hooks only.

## Family E — keep watch

### Setup

The heroine is told to remain awake and watch:
- a door;
- a cart;
- a storage room;
- a guest;
- a package.

### Scene draft

Nothing may happen.

That is part of the exhaustion.

Possible line:

### Variant pool

- rain at the door;
- silent warehouse;
- late wagon;
- nervous guest;
- locked office.

No event should invent a combat system or danger consequence.

## Family F — second set of records

### Setup

The visible ledger is not the only ledger.

At night, the heroine is made to copy or reconcile a private version.

### Scene draft

The numbers do not match the daytime books.

Nobody asks you to understand why.

### Future hooks

Potential:

- public fact for later authored route.

Not automatically granted.

## Family G — personal errand

### Setup

The hidden employer turns a work obligation into a personal convenience.

### Scene draft

The errand is trivial:
- fetch food;
- retrieve a coat;
- carry a letter;
- wait outside a residence;
- return an object.

Its mechanical weight comes only from the accepted forced-Night policy, not from the fiction.

### Tone

> The task could have waited.
>
> That is exactly why it matters that it did not.

## Family H — correction without violence

### Setup

The heroine is called in because:
- she argued;
- she was late;
- she asked a question;
- she resisted a daytime instruction.

### Scene draft

The scene focuses on control and warning rather than explicit physical harm.

Possible line:

> "You are confusing tolerance with permission."

Heroine response variants:
- silence;
- argument;
- dry compliance;
- direct refusal in tone only.

No branch effect is implied unless later authored.

## Family I — trusted late task

### Setup

Late-stage content may invert the tone.

The heroine is still compelled to attend, but the task now depends on her competence.

### Scene draft

> "I need this done correctly."
>
> For once, the second sentence matters more than the first.

Possible tasks:
- sensitive delivery;
- lockup;
- account check;
- meeting preparation;
- confidential message.

This may support later takeover narrative without becoming an automatic prerequisite.

## Family J — ambiguous kindness inside coercion

### Setup

A person connected to the hidden career leaves food, dry clothes, or a small comfort during a forced Night.

### Scene draft

The gesture is helpful.

It does not cancel the fact that the heroine had no choice about being there.

This supports relationship nuance without rewriting the underlying bound-state mechanics.

## Mechanical neutrality rule

Scene text must never silently decide:
- NightFlow intercept;
- whether lodging was already paid;
- whether lodging payment is suppressed;
- Energy cost;
- next-day Energy penalty;
- stat penalties;
- debt change;
- career counter gain;
- relationship facts;
- outcome eligibility.

All such effects require explicit production definitions.

## Day work separation

Never write forced Night as:
- "another shift" if runtime treats it as NightFlow;
- a substitute for daytime hidden work;
- ordinary career work;
- a source of ordinary Hard Work.

Authoring distinction:

Day work:
- chosen/executed as daytime hidden-career action under bound-state availability.

Forced Night:
- triggered by bound-state Night policy;
- mandatory;
- consumes/suppresses Night according to accepted Product policy.

## Variant selection guidance

Production conversion may use:
- hidden-career stage;
- public relationship fact;
- prior choice;
- season;
- housing context if Product allows it;
- event history.

Do not condition on guessed values.
