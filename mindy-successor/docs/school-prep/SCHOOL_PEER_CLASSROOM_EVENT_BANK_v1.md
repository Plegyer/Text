# School Peer / Classroom Event Bank v1

Status: CONTENT_DRAFT. Milestone-neutral reusable peer/classroom events for School.

These scenes add texture only. They do not create new prerequisites, relationship stages, public results, certificates, exams, projects, tuition, Energy, stat, Lust, or progression effects.

## Social friction

### The Wrong Correction
A student interrupts another with a confident correction that is only half-right. The room goes quiet. The teacher redirects the exchange by asking both students to explain what evidence would settle the disagreement.

Use:
- any theory-heavy or discussion-heavy class;
- teacher intervention variant;
- optional player response presentation.

### Borrowed Confidence
A student repeats the teacher’s phrasing almost word for word, then struggles when asked to explain it differently. The moment becomes a gentle lesson in the difference between sounding certain and understanding.

Use:
- Literacy & Numbers;
- Arcana;
- Rhetoric & Social;
- Etiquette & Presentation.

### The Competitive Answer
Two students race to answer first and both miss something important. The teacher slows the room down and asks what each answer noticed that the other ignored.

Use:
- all tracks where peer comparison makes sense.

## Collaboration

### Trade Notes
Two students compare notes and discover that each caught a different part of the lesson. Together, the result is clearer than either set alone.

### Paired Practice
The class divides into pairs with deliberately different strengths. The exercise works only when both people stop trying to do the entire task themselves.

### Explain It to Someone Else
One student understands the exercise but cannot explain it. Another can explain it but has not solved it. The teacher pairs them and lets the conversation do the work.

### Quiet Support
A student notices that someone nearby is stuck and offers a small hint rather than the answer. The teacher lets the interaction continue without turning it into a formal lesson.

## Embarrassment / recovery

### The Dropped Materials
A stack of notes, tools, or practice materials spills at the worst possible moment. The scene becomes about recovering calmly rather than pretending nothing happened.

### The Answer That Sounded Better Internally
A student gives a polished answer that falls apart halfway through. The teacher treats the attempt seriously and asks for the simpler version.

### Wrong Room Energy
A student arrives with exactly the wrong tone for the exercise: too loud, too formal, too casual, or too competitive. The teacher redirects without public humiliation.

### Unwanted Attention
A minor mistake attracts more attention than it deserves. Another student helps move the class forward before the moment becomes cruel.

## Success moments

### The Unexpected Solution
A student reaches the right result by an unusual route. The teacher checks the reasoning carefully before acknowledging that the method works.

### The Quiet Improvement
A student who usually struggles completes a routine step cleanly. The teacher notices without turning it into a spectacle.

### The Useful Question
Instead of giving an answer, a student asks the question that makes everyone else understand what the real problem is.

### Shared Win
A group exercise succeeds because several small contributions align. No single student can honestly claim the whole result.

## Teacher intervention

### Reset the Room
The class becomes distracted or tense. The teacher changes the format rather than raising the stakes: new pairs, shorter task, clearer objective.

### Make the Rule Explain Itself
A student challenges a rule as arbitrary. The teacher explains the problem the rule was meant to solve, then asks whether the rule still serves that purpose.

### Stop Before It Becomes Cruel
A joke lands badly. The teacher intervenes early, redirects the class, and preserves the embarrassed student’s dignity without turning the moment into a lecture.

### Turn the Mistake into Material
A failed attempt becomes the next exercise. The teacher removes blame and asks what information the failure provides.

## Track-specific peer seeds

### Literacy & Numbers
- students disagree over what a ledger entry actually means;
- one student writes clearly but calculates poorly, another the reverse;
- a copied notice changes meaning because of one ambiguous phrase.

### Arcana
- two students report different observations of the same harmless phenomenon;
- one student over-theorizes while another records too little;
- a practice diagram is correct but mislabeled.

### Rhetoric & Social
- two students pursue different goals in the same conversation;
- a joke wins the room but loses the argument;
- a quiet student notices a social assumption everyone else missed.

### Etiquette & Presentation
- a formal recovery is handled better than the original mistake;
- one student follows every rule while making another person uncomfortable;
- classmates compare what “prepared” looks like in different contexts.

### Field Craft
- two route plans prioritize different risks;
- a student notices a clue but misreads its importance;
- a cooperative task fails because nobody assigned roles.

### Physical Training
- partners have mismatched pacing;
- one student pushes too hard early and must adjust;
- a clean technical repetition outperforms a stronger sloppy one.

### Arts & Performance
- two interpretations of the same piece produce different but valid results;
- one performer copies another too closely;
- a mistake creates a better idea when the group keeps going.

### Body Confidence
- classmates choose different presentation routines for equally deliberate reasons;
- a student confuses discomfort with failure;
- someone practices stating a boundary clearly without escalating the scene.

## Reuse and production rules

Events from this bank may be adapted into:
- optional classroom offers;
- effect-free post-lesson presentation;
- teacher commentary;
- relationship-neutral peer moments;
- Night/social flavor where appropriate.

They must not:
- gate ordinary paid lessons;
- change School transaction mechanics;
- increment School counters;
- award relationship progress by default;
- publish world facts;
- imply milestone completion.

Any later relationship or career consequence must be authored separately through the correct domain boundary.
