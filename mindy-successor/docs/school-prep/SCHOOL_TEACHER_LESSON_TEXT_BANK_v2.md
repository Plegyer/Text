# School Teacher Lesson Text Bank v2

Status: CONTENT_DRAFT. Additional repeatable lesson copy for the eight existing drafted teacher identities.

This bank extends the v1 lesson text without changing tuition, Energy, stat packages, Lust behavior, School counters, prerequisites, milestones, or public-result semantics.

## Literacy & Numbers — Mara Fen

### Variant D — Bad Instructions
Mara hands you a set of directions that are technically correct and practically useless. She asks you to rewrite them for someone who has never seen the task before. “Accuracy that cannot be followed is only half-finished work.”

### Variant E — Estimate First
Before allowing exact calculation, Mara asks for a rough estimate. Your first answer is too precise for the evidence available. “Good arithmetic begins with knowing what kind of answer the situation deserves.”

### Variant F — Find the Assumption
A neat column of figures produces a suspiciously neat conclusion. Mara asks what assumption makes the total possible. The error turns out not to be arithmetic at all.

### Variant G — Explain It Back
Mara gives you a correct solution and asks you to teach it back in fewer words. Each revision removes a step that was decorative rather than necessary.

## Arcana — Ilyra Venn

### Variant D — Label the Unknown
Ilyra asks you to mark every part of an arcane diagram you do not understand. “Unnamed uncertainty hides inside confidence. Named uncertainty can be tested.”

### Variant E — Repeat the Observation
A weak magical response appears once and vanishes. Ilyra refuses to discuss meaning until you can reproduce the conditions that produced it.

### Variant F — Separate Cause from Timing
Two events happen close together in a practice setup. Ilyra asks you to prove that one caused the other rather than merely arriving first.

### Variant G — Record the Failure
A harmless trial produces nothing. Ilyra has you document the failed attempt with the same care as a success. “Future you deserves to know what not to repeat.”

## Rhetoric & Social — Cassian Reed

### Variant D — Ask Before Answering
Cassian gives you an angry complaint and forbids you from defending yourself immediately. The exercise ends only when you can ask a question that changes the shape of the conversation.

### Variant E — Cut the Clever Line
A polished sentence earns a laugh but loses the point. Cassian makes you remove it. “If the audience remembers the flourish and forgets the argument, you paid too much for applause.”

### Variant F — Read the Silence
Cassian pauses a mock conversation at the exact moment nobody speaks. He asks what each person in the room might be waiting for someone else to say.

### Variant G — Change Your Goal
Halfway through a persuasive exercise, Cassian changes the objective from winning agreement to preserving trust. Your wording changes with it.

## Etiquette & Presentation — Eliane Marrow

### Variant D — Recover Gracefully
Eliane has you make a small deliberate mistake in a formal exchange and practice recovering without turning the error into the center of attention.

### Variant E — Prepare the Room
Instead of correcting your clothes or posture, Eliane asks you to arrange the environment for another person’s comfort. “Presentation begins before anyone looks at you.”

### Variant F — Know What to Ignore
Eliane points out three tiny imperfections and asks which one actually matters. The lesson is as much about restraint as correction.

### Variant G — Formal Without Stiffness
A greeting becomes worse each time you overthink it. Eliane has you repeat it until the rule supports the interaction rather than replacing it.

## Field Craft — Rowan Pike

### Variant D — Leave a Trace
Rowan asks you to move through the practice area while deliberately leaving signs behind. On the return walk, you learn how obvious your passage looks from the other side.

### Variant E — Weather the Plan
A simple route suddenly changes when Rowan adds imagined rain and fading light. The original plan was not wrong; it was incomplete.

### Variant F — Check the Obvious
Rowan hides nothing at all. The exercise is to inspect the obvious places with the same discipline you would use for a clever clue.

### Variant G — Pack for the Problem
A small set of tools must serve three different tasks. Rowan makes you choose what to carry before telling you which task comes first.

## Physical Training — Tovan Kest

### Variant D — Stop One Early
Tovan ends a set one repetition before failure. “Training is not a contest against the floor. Leave enough quality to practice again.”

### Variant E — Breathe on Purpose
A simple drill becomes harder when your breathing loses rhythm. Tovan slows the pace until breath and movement work together.

### Variant F — Uneven Load
Tovan shifts the weight slightly off-center and asks you to stay controlled. The lesson is balance before force.

### Variant G — Recovery Test
The hardest part of the session is sitting still long enough to notice how quickly your breathing settles. Tovan calls recovery another measurement of training.

## Arts & Performance — Nessa Lark

### Variant D — Make It Too Much
Nessa asks you to exaggerate a choice past good taste. Once the intention becomes unmistakable, she helps you reduce it without losing clarity.

### Variant E — Use the Mistake
A missed cue becomes the center of the exercise. Nessa makes you incorporate it rather than restart. “Performance keeps moving even when the plan does not.”

### Variant F — Change the Audience
The same piece is performed for a child, a rival, and someone grieving. Nessa asks what must change and what must remain true.

### Variant G — Remove One Element
Nessa takes away a prop, gesture, or phrase you relied on. The gap forces you to discover what the scene was actually doing.

## Body Confidence — Sera Vey

### Variant D — Comfortable Attention
Sera asks you to stand where others can see you without performing for them. The exercise is simply to remain present without shrinking or exaggerating.

### Variant E — Choose the Routine
Several grooming steps are laid out, but Sera asks you to keep only the ones that serve your purpose. “A routine should belong to you.”

### Variant F — Name the Boundary
A social scenario asks for more familiarity than you want to give. Sera has you answer clearly without apology or unnecessary hostility.

### Variant G — Move Without Checking
You cross the room without stopping to inspect your reflection. Sera asks afterward what you noticed in your body instead of how you looked.
